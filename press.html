Bridget — press notes

September 2026 · free iPhone app · Seattle drawbridges

What it is

Bridget is a free iPhone app (tip-jar supported, no ads, no account, no servers of its own) that warns Seattle drivers when a drawbridge is backing up traffic. The only data that leaves your iPhone goes to Apple: Maps search, routes and traffic, and App Store tips. Four of the city's seven drawbridges can send alerts; all seven show on its board. It doesn't receive any official bridge signal: it watches the live drive time across each bridge's approach road and compares it to that road's empty-road drive time — what Apple Maps predicts for 3 a.m. A big slowdown means the bridge is backing up traffic — whether from an opening or ordinary congestion; Bridget watches the traffic, not the bridge. The alert thresholds are tuned against the City of Seattle's official drawbridge opening records — a one-time job done before the app ships; the app itself never reads those records. Full method: the methodology page.

The closure weekend it measured (Aug 21–23, 2026)

Three bridge approaches closed the same weekend: Montlake Blvd (SR 520 sign-structure work, from 10 PM Fri 8/21), northbound SR 99 at the 1st Ave S bridge (preservation work, Sat–Sun), and the Spokane St swing bridge (mechanical repairs). Bridget's calibration instrument — running a pre-registered two-week study at the time, meaning its pass/fail rules were written and frozen before the data existed — independently detected all three:

  • the first Montlake detection at 10:54 PM, 54 minutes after the posted 10 PM closure start;
  • every 1st Ave S detection northbound-only, matching the northbound-only closure;
  • a sustained Spokane St signature through the repair period.

Its response throughout was to decline to report travel numbers for roads it couldn't honestly measure — silence with reasons, never a confident wrong number.

The two-week study (Aug 17–31, 2026)

70,511 measurements over exactly 14 days, zero interruptions. Results: the flagship Ballard Bridge was retuned on 2.3× its previous data; and two bridges whose city status records went dark — University (no logged openings April 21 – Sept 2, 2026; three logged since, on Sept 2, 16 and 26) and Spokane St (since January 2026) — earned cautious, off-peak-only alerts, calibrated by matching each bridge's traffic-spike fingerprint to its own historical opening pattern. The method was tested blind first: hide a well-recorded bridge's answer key, ask the method to find its threshold from traffic alone, compare. It landed inside the accepted range on both test bridges.

A finding about the public record

By sampling the city's own live status page once a minute for 11 days and comparing it with the published opening log, the study measured how much the log misses: it captured about 98% of observed openings at Ballard, but only about 35% at South Park and about 7% at 1st Ave S.

Honest limits

Bridget reacts to congestion as it forms — it helps you avoid a jam; it does not predict a raise, and it can't promise a warning before a span goes up.
